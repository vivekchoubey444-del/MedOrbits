Code of Conduct

Our Commitment

MedOrbits is committed to maintaining a safe, respectful, inclusive, and constructive environment for everyone participating in its project and community spaces.

We welcome contributions from people of all experience levels, professional backgrounds, identities, and perspectives. Whether you are submitting code, reporting a bug, proposing a feature, reviewing a pull request, participating in a discussion, or helping improve documentation, your participation matters.

All participants are expected to act professionally, communicate respectfully, respect personal boundaries, and contribute in ways that support collaboration, learning, and the long-term quality of the project.

This Code of Conduct establishes the behavioral standards expected of everyone participating in the MedOrbits project.

1. Scope

This Code of Conduct applies to all participants in MedOrbits project spaces and to conduct connected with representing or participating in the project, including:

* GitHub issues, pull requests, discussions, reviews, and project repositories.
* Technical conversations, documentation contributions, and collaboration channels.
* Online meetings, community events, and project-related communications.
* Interactions where an individual is officially representing MedOrbits.
* Project-related communications outside official spaces when they materially affect participant safety or the integrity of the community.

These expectations apply equally to maintainers, administrators, contributors, reviewers, collaborators, and community members, regardless of seniority or access privileges.

2. Expected Behavior

Participants are expected to:

* Communicate respectfully: Be courteous, patient, professional, and constructive.
* Critique ideas, not people: Focus feedback on code, proposals, decisions, and observable behavior rather than personal attacks.
* Collaborate in good faith: Explain disagreements clearly, consider alternative approaches, and seek practical solutions.
* Use inclusive language: Avoid discriminatory, degrading, or exclusionary language.
* Respect boundaries: Accept that others may decline conversations, requests, or interactions.
* Protect privacy: Do not expose private communications, personal information, confidential records, credentials, or sensitive data without authorization.
* Give constructive feedback: Explain problems clearly and, where practical, suggest improvements.
* Respect project processes: Follow contribution guidelines, review procedures, security-reporting instructions, and reasonable moderation decisions.
* Acknowledge contributions: Give appropriate credit and avoid misrepresenting another person’s work.
* Act responsibly: Report genuine concerns through appropriate channels and avoid spreading unverified allegations.

Mistakes, technical disagreements, and good-faith differences of opinion are not automatically violations of this policy. They should be handled through respectful discussion and appropriate project procedures.

3. Unacceptable Behavior

The following conduct is prohibited in covered project spaces and interactions:

Harassment and discrimination

* Harassment, discrimination, intimidation, stalking, or threats.
* Unwelcome sexual attention, sexual harassment, or sexualized conduct directed at another participant.
* Abusive or degrading comments relating to personal characteristics or identity.
* Deliberate humiliation, personal insults, or repeated hostile targeting.

Disruptive and bad-faith conduct

* Trolling, sustained disruption, or repeated deliberate interference with constructive discussions.
* Repeatedly disregarding reasonable boundaries or moderation instructions.
* Misrepresenting another participant’s statements or contributions to cause harm.
* Deliberately obstructing reviews or collaboration through abusive or bad-faith behavior.

Privacy and security violations

* Publishing private information, confidential communications, or personal records without authorization.
* Sharing passwords, access tokens, private keys, session cookies, or other authentication secrets.
* Attempting unauthorized access to accounts, systems, repositories, or infrastructure.
* Disclosing private user data or exploiting a vulnerability in a manner that unnecessarily exposes people or systems to harm.
* Using project access or privileges to access, modify, or disclose information without authorization.

Retaliation and abuse of authority

* Retaliating against anyone who reports a concern in good faith, participates in an investigation, or provides relevant information.
* Abusing maintainer, administrative, review, or moderation privileges to intimidate, silence, or unfairly disadvantage others.
* Making knowingly false or malicious reports intended to harass or punish another participant.

Reasonable disagreement, good-faith reporting, respectful criticism of project decisions, and legitimate security research conducted within applicable authorization and disclosure rules are not prohibited merely because they are uncomfortable or critical.

4. Reporting a Concern

If you experience or witness conduct that may violate this Code of Conduct, report it privately to the MedOrbits project maintainers using a contact method published in the repository’s root README.md or on the official MedOrbits website.

Please include, where available and safe to share:

* A concise description of the incident.
* Relevant dates, times, repository links, issue references, or other context.
* The individuals or project spaces involved, where known.
* Any supporting evidence that can be shared safely.
* Whether there is an immediate safety, privacy, or security concern.
* Any action you believe may help resolve the situation.

You do not need to provide every detail or confront the person involved before making a report.

Protect sensitive information. Do not include passwords, access tokens, private keys, identity documents, unrelated personal information, or unnecessary copies of confidential records.

If no private reporting contact is published, request a private reporting channel from a maintainer before sharing sensitive details. Do not submit sensitive allegations through a public issue or pull request.

For suspected security vulnerabilities, use the dedicated process documented in SECURITY.md. Do not disclose exploitable details publicly before following that process.

For urgent threats or immediate danger, contact the appropriate local emergency or safety authorities where necessary. Project moderation is not a substitute for emergency assistance.

5. Report Handling and Confidentiality

Maintainers should handle reports promptly, impartially, and with appropriate discretion.

Where practical, the handling process should include:

1. Acknowledgment: Confirm receipt of the report when a safe response channel is available.
2. Initial assessment: Evaluate the reported conduct, potential risk, and any immediate protective measures needed.
3. Information gathering: Review relevant evidence and request additional information where necessary.
4. Fair consideration: Consider the available information, context, and responses from relevant participants where appropriate.
5. Decision: Determine whether a policy violation occurred and select a proportionate response.
6. Communication: Inform relevant parties of the outcome to the extent appropriate and safe.
7. Follow-up: Consider whether additional measures are necessary to prevent recurrence or retaliation.

Reports will be handled confidentially to the extent reasonably practical. Absolute confidentiality cannot be guaranteed because reviewing a report may require sharing limited information with relevant people or responding to legal obligations.

Maintainers should limit access to reports and supporting evidence to those who need the information to assess or address the concern.

6. Enforcement and Possible Consequences

Maintainers are responsible for applying this Code of Conduct fairly and consistently.

Depending on the nature, severity, frequency, impact, and context of the conduct, possible responses include:

1. Informal guidance: A private request to clarify expectations or correct minor conduct.
2. Formal warning: A clear notice that the behavior violates this policy and must stop.
3. Content moderation: Removal, editing, locking, or restriction of harmful content or discussions where permitted.
4. Temporary restrictions: Temporary limits on commenting, reviewing, contributing, or participating in project spaces.
5. Suspension: Temporary suspension of participation or project privileges.
6. Permanent exclusion: Removal of project access or a permanent ban from covered community spaces where permitted.

The response should be proportionate to the circumstances. Serious threats, harassment, unauthorized disclosure, or abuse of privileged access may warrant immediate protective action without prior informal warnings.

Where practical, maintainers should document significant enforcement decisions and explain the relevant expectations to affected participants. Private information and details that could expose a reporter or another participant to harm should not be disclosed unnecessarily.

7. Appeals and Review

A participant who believes an enforcement decision was made in error may request a review through a private channel.

An appeal should explain the decision being challenged, the reason for the challenge, and any relevant additional information.

Where practical, the review should be handled by a maintainer or authorized reviewer who was not primarily responsible for the original decision. If no independent reviewer is available, the project should communicate that limitation.

An appeal does not automatically suspend an enforcement action. Interim restrictions may remain in place while a review is pending when necessary to protect participants or project systems.

Abusive appeals or attempts to intimidate reporters, witnesses, or reviewers are not acceptable.

8. Responsibilities of Maintainers

Maintainers and administrators are expected to:

* Communicate and apply these standards consistently.
* Respond to reports in a timely and appropriate manner, considering severity and available resources.
* Protect sensitive reporting information and restrict access where practical.
* Avoid conflicts of interest when assessing reports.
* Take reasonable steps to prevent retaliation.
* Use project privileges responsibly and only for legitimate project purposes.
* Explain moderation decisions when appropriate and safe.
* Review this policy periodically and update it as the project evolves.

Maintainers are not expected to disclose private investigation details or guarantee a particular outcome. However, reports should not be ignored solely because the person involved is a contributor, reviewer, administrator, or maintainer.

9. Good-Faith Reporting and Anti-Retaliation

MedOrbits encourages good-faith reporting of misconduct and cooperation with reasonable reviews.

No participant should be threatened, harassed, punished, or unfairly excluded for making a good-faith report, providing relevant evidence, requesting help, or participating in an investigation.

Knowingly fabricated reports or deliberate misuse of the reporting process may themselves be addressed under this policy. An unsubstantiated report is not, by itself, evidence of dishonesty or misconduct.

10. Relationship to Other Project Policies

This Code of Conduct operates alongside the project’s other policies and documentation, including:

* CONTRIBUTING.md — contribution requirements and development workflows.
* SECURITY.md — security vulnerability reporting and disclosure procedures.
* LICENSE — the terms governing use and distribution of project materials.
* README.md — project information and official contact channels.

Where another policy establishes a more specific procedure for a particular matter, participants should follow that procedure in addition to the behavioral expectations described here.

This Code of Conduct does not replace applicable law, contractual obligations, or formal security and privacy requirements.

11. Policy Updates

MedOrbits may revise this Code of Conduct as its community, technology, and operational needs evolve.

Material changes should be documented through the project’s normal version-control and review processes. Continued participation in project spaces is expected to follow the currently published policy.
