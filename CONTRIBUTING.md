Security Policy

Supported Versions

Security fixes are generally prioritized for the currently deployed or actively maintained version of MedOrbits. Unless a release or support policy says otherwise, older versions may not receive security updates.

|Version                       |Supported|
|——————————|———|
|Current maintained version    |Yes      |
|Older or unmaintained versions|No       |

Reporting a Vulnerability

Please do not report security vulnerabilities in public issues, discussions, or social media.

Until a dedicated security contact is published, report a suspected vulnerability privately to the project maintainers using a contact method listed in the repository’s README or the official MedOrbits website. Include “Security Vulnerability Report” in the subject line where possible.

Please do not assume that a contact address exists unless it is explicitly published in those locations. If no private contact method is available, ask a maintainer through a non-public channel to provide one before sharing sensitive technical details.

What to Include

To help us investigate, include as much of the following as you can safely provide:

• A clear description of the suspected vulnerability and its potential impact.
• Affected page, endpoint, component, version, or configuration.
• Reproduction steps or a minimal proof of concept.
• Relevant logs, timestamps, and request/response details with secrets and personal data removed.
• Any suggested mitigation, if known.

Do not include passwords, API keys, session tokens, payment card data, identity documents, or other sensitive personal information in a report.

What to Expect

The maintainers will review the report, attempt to validate the issue, and determine its severity and remediation priority. We aim to acknowledge reports within 5 business days and provide status updates as practical. These are response goals, not a guarantee.

Please allow reasonable time for investigation and remediation before public disclosure. We ask reporters not to exploit the issue beyond what is necessary to demonstrate it, access or modify other people’s data, disrupt services, or publicly disclose details before coordinating with the maintainers.

Scope and Safe Harbor

Only test systems that you own or have explicit authorization to assess. Do not perform denial-of-service testing, social engineering, physical attacks, destructive testing, or tests that expose, alter, or retain other users’ data.

This policy does not grant permission to violate applicable law, access data without authorization, or disrupt MedOrbits or third-party services.

Disclosure and Credit

After a fix is available, the maintainers may coordinate a public advisory with the reporter. Credit may be provided if requested and appropriate, but it is not guaranteed. Please obtain consent before publishing personal information about a reporter.

Policy Maintenance

This policy should be reviewed whenever the project publishes a dedicated security contact, changes its supported-version policy, or establishes a formal vulnerability disclosure process.
